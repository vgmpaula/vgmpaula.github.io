01 / ABOUT

A curious kind of chemistry

Science is a way of seeing things differently.

I study how materials transform and how knowledge should transform with them. My work connects green chemistry, separation engineering, material science, and physical-chemistry to rethink what happens to polymers after their first life.

From the interactions that determine whether a solvent works to the processes that make recovery practical, I'm interested in the space where fundamental understanding becomes a better material decision.

02 / RESEARCH

Choose the intervention.

What is the least intensive intervention that retains the greatest material value?

My work follows three connected acts of polymer circularity in a 5S framework: preserve what still has value (Selective Solvation), recover building blocks when preservation is no longer appropriate (Solvolysis), and design the next material with its future in mind (Synthesis).

ACT I / SELECTIVE SOLVATIONI
Preserve molecular value

Don't break what you don't need to.

Selective solvents separate and recover polymers from complex waste without breaking its polymer backbone.

ENGINEERING THERMOPLASTICS · SELECTIVE DISSOLUTION
ACT II / SOLVOLYSISII
Recover molecular value

If you break it, break intelligently.

When chains must be broken, polyesters can return to useful building blocks — and those building blocks can become polymers again.

POLYESTERS · DEPOLYMERIZATION
ACT III / SUSTAINABLE SYNTHESISIII
Design the next lifecycle

When you build, build differently.

Renewable furan-based polyesters invite a bigger question: can feedstock, synthesis and end-of-life be designed together?

FURANIC POLYESTERS · GREENER SYNTHESIS
✳

Preserve when possible. Rebuild when necessary. Design for what comes next. The chemistry should follow a grave-to-cradle design logic, not the other way around.
